Signs to Watch

What Are the Signs Your New Orleans Lawn Needs a Professional?

Your New Orleans lawn needs a professional when the grass keeps getting ahead of the mower, when the turf under trees is turning thin and patchy, or when weeds are flowering along beds and fences. Each of those gets harder to fix the longer it waits, especially in summer.

Clumps After Every Cut

Grass left to grow far past its height leaves heavy clumps behind the mower. LSU AgCenter advises never removing more than a third of the blade at once, so an overgrown lawn has to come down in stages.

Bare Ground Under Trees

Thin, bare patches under a canopy usually mean the grass is short on light. A taller cut helps, and the deepest shade may do better as a mulched bed.

Weeds Going to Seed

Weeds that flower along fences and bed edges drop seed for next season. Cutting them back before they seed means fewer weeds the following year.

Local Conditions

Why Do New Orleans Lawns Need a Different Approach?

New Orleans lawns need a different approach because so many grow under mature live oaks, on lots shaped by three centuries of building along the river. St. Augustine copes with shade better than most warm-season grasses, but it still needs a taller cut and a lighter hand under trees to stay thick and green.

  • Oaks shade a lot of lawns.Oak trees shade older neighborhoods such as the Garden District, and LSU AgCenter treats less than four hours of direct sun as high shade stress for turf.
  • The oldest streets sit on high ground.Early neighborhoods grew along the river's natural levee, while many later ones were laid out on drained swampland.
  • Housing runs from cottages to mansions.Creole cottages, Victorian houses, and Garden District mansions each leave a very different amount of yard around the house.
  • One city, one parish.New Orleans and Orleans Parish share a single boundary, set out in the city's Home Rule Charter.

FAQ

What Do People Ask About Lawn Service in New Orleans?

People who ask about lawn service in New Orleans mostly want to know how local grasses behave: how St. Augustine handles oak shade and winter, how soon a lawn can be cut after rain, why grass turns yellow, and what a visit should cost. These are the questions we hear most.

does st augustine grass grow in shade?

Yes, within limits: LSU AgCenter rates St. Augustine as more shade tolerant than other warm-season grasses, but it will not grow well in dense shade. Under New Orleans live oaks, keep it cut at 3 inches or more, and consider mulch or a ground cover where the lawn gets under four hours of direct sun.

does st augustine grass go dormant?

Yes, because St. Augustine is a warm-season grass with a low tolerance for cold, so growth slows and the lawn goes largely dormant through the cooler months in New Orleans. LSU AgCenter notes that south Louisiana lawns may show signs of green-up in late February, and regular mowing picks up again from there.

how soon after it rains can i mow the lawn?

Wait until the grass blades are reasonably dry. LSU AgCenter advises against mowing a wet lawn, and wet grass also tends to clump behind the mower. After a New Orleans downpour, how long that takes depends on sun, shade, and how quickly the yard drains, so we check the lawn before we cut.

why is my st augustine grass turning yellow?

Two common causes are low iron and low nitrogen. LSU AgCenter notes that iron deficiency yellows the younger leaves between the veins, while nitrogen deficiency starts in the older leaves. Cool, wet springs and high soil pH can both limit iron, so a soil test is the best next step.

how much water does st augustine grass need?

In a normal year, rainfall alone may keep St. Augustine healthy in New Orleans, according to LSU AgCenter. During dry spells, water deeply so the root zone is moistened several inches down rather than sprinkling a little every day, and water in the early morning to help limit disease.

when should you mow your lawn after winter?

Start when the lawn has clearly greened up and begun growing again, which LSU AgCenter says can be as early as late February in south Louisiana. Never remove more than a third of the blade at once, and hold off on fertilizer until mid-April, when LSU says feeding should begin in south Louisiana.

is my grass bermuda or st augustine?

Look closely at the leaf: LSU AgCenter describes St. Augustine as coarse, with wide leaves that end in a rounded tip, while Bermuda has narrow, sharply pointed leaves and a finer texture. Bermuda also needs full sun, so a New Orleans lawn that is thriving in partial shade is unlikely to be Bermuda.

how much should i pay someone to mow my lawn?

What you pay depends on the size of the lawn, how often it is cut, how overgrown it is, and whether trimming, edging, or cleanup is included. In New Orleans, the clearest number comes from an on-site look, which is why we offer a free consultation and a no-obligation quote first.
