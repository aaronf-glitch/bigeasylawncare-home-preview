Common Lawn Trimming Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

February 21, 2025·4 min read·By the Big Easy Lawn Care team

Key Takeaway

Maintaining a well-kept lawn requires more than just occasional mowing. Lawn trimming is a crucial part of yard care, but it’s also an area where many people go wrong. By understanding common mistakes and adopting better habits, you can make your lawn care routine more effective and keep your yard looking its best.

Taking care of your lawn can feel like a simple task, but even small mistakes in trimming can lead to bigger issues over time. From uneven grass to poor lawn health, improper trimming habits can cost you time, effort, and money. To help you achieve a lush and healthy yard, we at Big Easy Lawn Care compiled a guide on the most common lawn-trimming mistakes and how to avoid them.

Mistake #1: Trimming Too Short

Why It’s a Problem:

Cutting grass too short, also known as “scalping,” weakens the lawn by exposing the soil and roots to the sun. This can lead to weed growth and damage from pests.

How to Avoid It:

Stick to the one-third rule: never cut more than one-third of the grass height in a single mowing session. Different grass types have recommended heights, so adjust your mower accordingly.

Mistake #2: Using Dull Mower Blades

Why It’s a Problem:

Dull blades tear the grass instead of cutting it cleanly. This can cause brown tips, increase the risk of disease, and stress the lawn.

How to Avoid It:

Sharpen your mower blades at least once a season or after every 10-12 hours of mowing. Keeping your blades sharp ensures a clean cut that promotes healthy growth.

Mistake #3: Mowing Wet Grass

Why It’s a Problem:

Wet grass can clump together, causing uneven cuts and clogging your mower. Additionally, mowing wet grass can compact the soil, leading to poor grass growth.

How to Avoid It:

Wait until the grass is dry before mowing. If you live in a rainy area, consider mowing later in the day when the grass has had time to dry.

Mistake #4: Ignoring Grass Type and Growth Patterns

Why It’s a Problem:

Not all grass types grow the same way or require the same care. Ignoring these differences can result in a patchy and uneven lawn.

How to Avoid It:

Learn about the type of grass in your lawn and tailor your trimming practices to its specific needs. For example, cool-season grasses like fescue grow differently from warm-season grasses like Bermuda.

Mistake #5: Not Following a Regular Mowing Schedule

Why It’s a Problem:

Irregular mowing allows the grass to grow too tall, making it harder to trim evenly. It also puts stress on the lawn when you cut it back too aggressively.

How to Avoid It:

Mow your lawn consistently based on the season and the growth rate of your grass. During peak growing months, you may need to mow more frequently, while in cooler months, you can reduce mowing frequency.

Mistake #6: Neglecting the Edges of the Lawn

Why It’s a Problem:

Overgrown edges can make the entire lawn look untidy and provide a breeding ground for weeds.

How to Avoid It:

Use an edger or trimmer to clean up the borders of your lawn after mowing. This creates a neat and polished appearance that enhances the overall look of your yard.

Mistake #7: Overlooking Lawn Mower Maintenance

Why It’s a Problem:

A poorly maintained mower can lead to uneven cuts, frequent breakdowns, and even damage to your lawn.

How to Avoid It:

Regularly check and maintain your mower. Clean the blades, change the oil, and inspect the wheels for wear and tear. Proper maintenance keeps your mower in good condition and ensures optimal performance.

Keep Your Lawn Healthy by Avoiding Common Trimming Mistakes

Taking care of your lawn doesn’t have to be complicated, but it does require attention to detail. Avoiding common trimming mistakes—like mowing too short, using dull blades, and neglecting your mower—can make a significant difference in your lawn’s health and appearance. By following proper trimming practices and staying consistent, you’ll enjoy a beautiful, green lawn that enhances your home’s curb appeal.

In New Orleans, our long growing season and Gulf humidity keep lawns growing nearly year-round, which means more frequent mowing and trimming than cooler climates. We tailor every visit to how local grasses like St. Augustine actually grow here.
