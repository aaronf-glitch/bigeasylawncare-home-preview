How to Prepare Your Yard for Hurricane Season in New Orleans, LA

August 24, 2026·8 min read·By the Big Easy Lawn Care team

Preparing a New Orleans yard for hurricane season means removing what wind can pick up, clearing the paths water needs to leave, and having a certified arborist deal with anything overhanging a structure. Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30, and the work belongs at the start of it rather than in the days before a named storm, when crews are booked and debris pickup has stopped.

At Big Easy Lawn Care, we field the hardest conversation of the season in the 48 hours before landfall, when a homeowner wants limb work done and no crew is available. Preparation is a June task, not a forecast-week task.

A great deal of avoidable storm damage starts in the yard. Loose objects, blocked drainage, and untended trees account for much of it.

This guide covers what the season actually does to a yard, what to look at, and how the work gets scheduled. Contact us today to get your property on a pre-season schedule.

What Hurricane Season Means for a New Orleans Yard

Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30, and a New Orleans yard faces three distinct pressures across those months. They are worth separating, because the preparation for each is different.

Wind and Loose Objects

Wind damage to a house frequently starts with something that was sitting in the yard. Furniture, planters, grills, and yard decor all become airborne well below the wind speeds a major storm brings.

The exposure is not limited to your own property either. An object that leaves your yard can damage a neighbor’s home, which turns a preparation lapse into a liability conversation.

Water With Nowhere to Go

New Orleans drainage depends on a pumping system, and heavy rainfall regularly outpaces it. When that happens, the only water leaving your yard is what the yard itself can route away.

Blocked drains, clogged gutter outlets, and beds graded toward the house all reduce that capacity. Those are yard-level problems with house-level consequences.

Saturated ground compounds it, since soil already holding water from earlier rain cannot absorb much more.

Trees and Certified Arborist Work

Trees are a common source of serious storm damage in residential settings, and much of that damage comes from limbs rather than whole trees. Dead wood, overextended branches, and limbs crossing a roofline are the recurring culprits.

This is the one category that is never a homeowner task. Anything overhanging a structure or near a utility line belongs to a certified arborist with the equipment and insurance to handle it.

Common Yard Hazards Before a Storm

Most yards carry the same handful of hazards, and most of them are fixable in a weekend or a single service visit.

Dead and Overhanging Limbs

Dead limbs come down in ordinary thunderstorms, let alone tropical systems. Any limb over a roof, a driveway, a fence line, or a power service drop is a priority item.

Have a certified arborist assess the tree and do the cutting. Ladder work with a saw near a structure or a line is how homeowners get seriously hurt every season.

Unsecured Furniture and Decor

Patio furniture, umbrellas, planters, grills, trash bins, and lawn ornaments all need a plan before a watch is issued. The plan is either bring it inside or anchor it properly, and “it has always been fine” is not a plan.

Decide now where each item goes and whether it fits. Discovering the shed is full while a storm is 36 hours out is a bad time to solve it.

Blocked Drains and Gutter Outlets

Yard drains, catch basins, and the point where downspouts discharge all silt up quietly over months. A drain that still moves ordinary rain can be well below the capacity it had when installed.

Clear them at the start of the season and again mid-season. Our clean up and hauling service handles the accumulated yard debris that ends up in those inlets.

Overgrown Beds Against the House

Dense plantings against a wall trap moisture, hide drainage problems, and give wind something to push against the structure. Overgrowth also conceals the foundation line where you would notice pooling.

Cut beds back from the wall and keep the base of the house visible. Our lawn trimming service keeps the surrounding grass and yard edges from closing back in on that space.

Warning Signs Your Yard Is Not Ready

These signs show up during ordinary weather, which is exactly why they are useful. A yard that struggles with a normal afternoon thunderstorm will not cope with a tropical system.

Standing Water That Lingers

Water still sitting in the yard a day after ordinary rain means drainage capacity is already exhausted. That is the baseline the storm will build on.

Note where it collects and how long it stays, since those two facts tell a contractor more than any description of the yard. Repeat locations matter more than one-off puddles, so a spot that reappears after every rain is the one worth acting on.

Gutter Overflow During Ordinary Rain

Gutters spilling over the front edge in a routine shower are either clogged or undersized for the roof. Either way, the water is landing at the foundation instead of being carried away.

Overflow is also the cheapest diagnostic you have. It is visible from the ground, and it tells you the system is not working before a storm proves it.

Loose or Rotting Fence Sections

A fence panel that moves when you push it will not survive sustained wind, and a failed panel becomes debris. Rot at the post base is the usual failure point in our humidity.

Check each post by hand at the start of the season. Repairing two panels in June is a different job than replacing a fence in September.

Gates deserve the same attention, and a latch that no longer catches cleanly is often the first sign a post has started to move.

How Yard Preparation Is Scheduled

Timing matters as much as the work itself, because availability collapses once a storm is named. The table below is the sequence we recommend to New Orleans property owners.

Task When to Handle It Why the Timing Matters
Certified arborist assessment and limb work Early in the season, before June if possible Arborists book out fastest and cannot work safely in wind. This is the item that becomes impossible last.
Clearing yard drains, catch basins, and downspout outlets Start of season, repeated mid-season Inlets silt up steadily. One clearing in June is already partly undone by September.
Cutting beds back off the house and clearing overgrowth Start of season, with routine maintenance after Keeps the foundation line visible so drainage problems stay findable all season.
Deciding where loose items go and testing that it works Once, at the start of the season The plan has to be rehearsed. An untested plan fails at the moment it is needed.
Fence, gate, and post inspection Start of season Repairs need dry weather and lead time on materials. Failed panels become airborne debris.

What This Means for Your New Orleans Property

Nearly all of this is ordinary maintenance moved earlier in the calendar, and the yard work that protects a house is the same work that keeps it looking cared for. The only genuine specialist item is the tree assessment, and that is the one worth booking first.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does hurricane season start and end?

Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30 each year. Yard preparation belongs at the start of that window rather than when a storm is forecast, because contractor availability drops sharply once a system is named and tree crews in particular book out well in advance.

What should I do with patio furniture before a hurricane?

Bring it indoors, into a garage, or into a shed. Anchoring is a second-best option and only works with hardware rated for the load. Decide where every item will go at the start of the season and confirm the space actually exists, since discovering storage is full during a watch wastes the preparation window.

Should I trim my trees before hurricane season?

Yes, and it should be done by a certified arborist rather than by a homeowner. Dead wood, overextended branches, and limbs crossing a roofline are the recurring sources of storm damage. Ladder work with a saw near a structure or a utility line is genuinely dangerous and is not a do-it-yourself task.

How do I keep my yard from flooding during a storm?

Clear yard drains, catch basins, and downspout outlets before the season and again partway through, since inlets silt up steadily. Check that beds and grade direct water away from the house rather than toward it. Water still standing a day after ordinary rain signals capacity that is already exhausted.

Is it too late to prepare if a storm is already forecast?

Some items still help, such as securing loose objects and clearing visible debris from drains. Anything requiring a contractor is usually out of reach by then, because crews are booked and tree work cannot be performed safely in rising wind. Treat forecast week as execution of an existing plan, not as the time to make one.

Who is responsible if my yard debris damages a neighbor’s property?

Liability depends on the circumstances and on your insurance, so it is worth asking your carrier directly rather than assuming. What is clear is that securing loose items and maintaining trees are the practical steps within your control. Documented maintenance is generally better than none if a claim arises.

Do I need to cut back plants against my house?

It helps in several ways. Dense plantings against a wall trap moisture, conceal drainage problems at the foundation line, and give wind something to push against the structure. Keeping beds cut back from the wall also means you can actually see pooling or grade issues while there is still time to fix them.

How often should yard drains be cleared in New Orleans?

At least at the start of hurricane season and once again partway through, with extra attention after heavy leaf drop or any storm that moves debris around. Drains and catch basins accumulate silt and organic matter continuously, so a drain that handled last year’s rain may carry noticeably less now.
