Signs to Watch

What Are the Warning Signs a Mandeville Lawn Needs a Pro?

Three warning signs tell you a Mandeville lawn needs professional care: green moss spreading where turf used to grow, a mat of pine needles and oak leaves that never gets cleared, and fire ant mounds that return after every treatment. Planned visits catch each of these before it spreads.

Moss Where Grass Was

LSU AgCenter notes that moss does well in shady, wet, compacted, or low-fertility soil with thin turf. Spreading moss usually means the grass has lost ground to the conditions, not just to a missed mowing.

Litter Left on the Lawn

LSU AgCenter lists fallen leaves left to pile up as an extra stress on shaded turf. Its advice is to collect the leaves rather than let them mat down on grass already short on light.

Mounds That Keep Coming

Fire ant mounds that rise again after you treat them suggest the colony survived. LSU AgCenter recommends a broadcast bait in spring followed by mound treatments, then repeating both steps in the fall.

FAQ

What Do Mandeville Residents Ask About Lawn Service?

Mandeville residents asking about lawn service usually want help with the problems trees and rain create: grass under oaks, leaves left on the turf, watering under a canopy, and new sod. They also ask about timing, such as when weed preventers go down, and where Mandeville sits in the parish.

what parish is mandeville la in?

Mandeville is in St. Tammany Parish, on the north shore of Lake Pontchartrain, and it is the first parish town drivers reach after crossing the Causeway from the south shore. We serve Mandeville along with its parish neighbors Covington, Madisonville, and Abita Springs, so one call covers yards across that part of the Northshore.

how far is mandeville from new orleans?

Mandeville sits at the north end of the Lake Pontchartrain Causeway, a bridge that spans nearly 24 miles over the lake, so most of the trip is over water. The exact distance depends on where in New Orleans you start. Our lawn service covers both sides of the lake, from the city up to Mandeville.

how to grow grass under oak trees?

Give the grass every advantage it can get: LSU AgCenter says lawn grasses want about five hours of direct sun, suggests St. Augustine for shade, and recommends a 3 to 3 1/2 inch cut there. Under the big oaks common in Mandeville, raising low limbs adds light, and mulch beds suit the darkest spots.

should you rake leaves off your lawn?

Yes, especially in shade. LSU AgCenter lists fallen leaves left to pile up as an extra stress on shaded turf, and its advice is to collect them rather than let them mat down on grass already short on light. Under Mandeville oaks and pines, rake litter off often and reuse the needles as pine straw in your beds.

when to apply pre emergent?

LSU AgCenter says crabgrass seed starts to sprout when soil reaches 55 to 60 F, around mid-February in south Louisiana, so it suggests applying a preemergence herbicide around Valentine's Day. Mandeville falls in that southern window. Winters vary from year to year, so watch the forecast, and always follow the product label when you apply.

how often to water new sod?

LSU AgCenter advises watering new sod one to two times a day for the first 10 to 14 days while the grass is actively growing. After that, water less often but for longer, which pushes roots deeper. Northshore rain can cover some of those waterings, so check the soil before running sprinklers.

when to lay sod?

LSU AgCenter calls April to October the ideal window for laying sod in Louisiana. Sod can go down year-round, but sod laid while dormant, from October to March, is more likely to dry out or suffer winter injury. In shaded Mandeville yards, keep in mind that sod is grown in full sun and may thin under trees.

does grass under trees need more water?

Often, yes. LSU AgCenter warns that tree roots crowd into the same upper soil as turf roots and take water and nutrients, so grass under trees may need more irrigation than turf in the open. In shaded Mandeville yards, check the soil under the canopy during dry spells instead of assuming the whole lawn needs the same amount.
