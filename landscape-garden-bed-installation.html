Local Conditions

Why Do Garden Beds Need Good Drainage in New Orleans?

Garden beds need good drainage in New Orleans because many soils in our area drain poorly after heavy rain, and roots sitting in wet soil struggle to establish. LSU AgCenter calls proper soil pH and internal drainage very important in a landscape bed, so we plan each bed around our climate and soil.

  • Wet ground needs height.LSU AgCenter calls raised beds almost essential for landscape plants when French drains or berms are not used.
  • Single plants get a mound.For one tree or shrub, LSU AgCenter suggests a berm about 1 foot tall that slopes gently back to the surrounding soil.
  • pH guides the soil.LSU AgCenter puts the ideal pH for most ornamental plants in Louisiana at 5.5 to 6.5, adjusted only after a soil test.
Bed Types

Should a Garden Bed Be Raised or In-Ground in New Orleans?

In New Orleans, a raised garden bed is the safer choice wherever soil stays wet after rain, and LSU AgCenter recommends building one at least 12 inches deep. On well-drained ground, an in-ground bed works well once the turf is removed, the soil is loosened, and organic matter is worked in.

In-Ground Bed

Built up and enclosed with brick, concrete edging, or wood, it lifts roots above soil that holds water after storms.

Raised Rows

FAQ

What Questions Do We Get About Garden Bed Installation in New Orleans?

how to prepare a garden bed?

Start by removing all existing grass and weeds, then till or fork the soil to loosen it. LSU AgCenter recommends spreading about 2 inches of organic matter, such as compost, peat moss, or rotted manure, and working it in. Add 2 to 3 inches of blended garden soil if the bed needs more height.

how to make a garden bed in the ground?

Clear the grass, then till the area about 8 inches deep. For vegetables, LSU AgCenter describes moving soil from the walkways up into rows raised 8 to 12 inches, then working in a 2- to 4-inch layer of organic matter. In New Orleans, that extra height helps the bed drain in heavy rain.

how deep should a raised garden bed be?

LSU AgCenter recommends making a raised landscape bed at least 12 inches deep where the ground drains poorly. It also describes 12-inch-high vegetable beds with sides of boards, cinder blocks, or similar materials. That depth gives roots room to grow above native soil that stays wet after heavy rain.

what to fill a raised garden bed with?

For vegetables, LSU AgCenter suggests an equal mix of sand, compost, and garden loam, or a bagged raised bed mix for small beds. Its raised bed guide calls for a loamy, well-drained vegetable blend at pH 6.0 to 7.0, while most ornamental beds do best at 5.5 to 6.5. Avoid too much bark, which can tie up nitrogen.

how to layer a raised garden bed?

Clear the grass and weeds first, by hand or by smothering them with cardboard or newspaper, then rake the ground clean. LSU AgCenter suggests a layer of weed barrier fabric and cardboard inside the frame before the soil goes in, followed by the soil mix and a top layer of mulch.

how often to water raised garden bed?

Raised beds need water more often than in-ground beds, because LSU AgCenter notes they dry out quickly during hot Louisiana summers. The same LSU guidance recommends drip irrigation or soaker hoses to keep soil moisture consistent. In New Orleans, watch new beds most closely through the summer heat.

how to edge a garden bed?

A broad edging strip keeps lawn grass from creeping into a bed. LSU AgCenter lists bricks laid flat, flagstone, a strip of bare ground, or a heavy layer of mulch such as wood chips or bark. A clean edge also gives the mower a clear line to follow.

what is a raised garden bed?

A raised garden bed is a planting area built up above the surrounding ground, usually enclosed with wood, brick, or concrete block and filled with a prepared soil mix. LSU AgCenter notes raised beds warm up and dry out faster after rain, which makes them a practical choice where soil stays wet.
