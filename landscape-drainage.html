Local Conditions

Why Does Yard Drainage Matter So Much in New Orleans?

Yard drainage matters in New Orleans because the city's drainage system starts at your roof and runs mostly on gravity, through streets and catch basins to pump stations. The Sewerage and Water Board notes that rain falling faster than about an inch an hour will most likely outpace that system, so each yard has to shed water well.

  • The system starts on your roof.Runoff moves from roofs into the streets, then through catch basins, pipes, and canals to the pump stations.
  • Pumps wait for gravity.The Sewerage and Water Board says its pumps cannot be activated until storm water reaches them by gravity.
  • Street flooding goes to 311.Flooded streets and blocked catch basins are reported to the Sewerage and Water Board through 311 or its FloodTracker.
Drainage Fixes

What Fixes Yard Drainage Problems in New Orleans?

Most yard drainage problems in New Orleans are fixed by giving water a slope to follow and a clear way out. That means grading soil and beds away from the house, clearing silted drains and downspout outlets, loosening compacted soil so rain can soak in, and matching new sod to the soil it is laid on.

FAQ

What Are Common Yard Drainage Questions in New Orleans?

who to call for drainage problems in yard?

Call a landscaping company that handles drainage when water stands on your own property, and call 311 for street flooding or a blocked catch basin. In New Orleans, the Sewerage and Water Board takes those reports through 311 and its online FloodTracker. Constantly soggy ground with a foul smell can point to a drainage problem or even hidden plumbing issues.

how to grade a yard for drainage?

Shape the soil so the ground falls away from the house and toward the street or another outlet, with no dips where water can sit. LSU AgCenter advises evaluating the drainage pattern first, contouring the area during site preparation, then setting the final grade and lightly rolling it so the surface holds its shape.

how to fix drainage issues in yard?

Start by finding where the water collects and how long it stays after an ordinary rain. Then fix the cause: regrade beds and low spots that tilt toward the house, clear silted yard drains and downspout outlets, and loosen compacted soil. A spot that stays wet after every rain usually needs grading or a drainage system.

will aerating lawn help drainage?

Aeration can help when the problem is compacted soil, because opening up the surface lets water soak in instead of running off or pooling. It will not fix a yard that is graded toward the house or a low spot with no outlet, so check the slope of the ground before relying on aeration alone.

how to improve soil drainage in lawn?

Relieve compaction with core aeration, keep heavy traffic off wet ground, and avoid overwatering. When laying new sod, LSU AgCenter warns that sod grown on a different soil, such as clay sod on sandy ground, can affect water infiltration and drainage, so match the sod to the site.

how to dry up a wet yard fast without drainage?

Without a drainage fix, the quickest steps are to stop adding water and give what is there a path out. Turn off irrigation, point downspouts away from the soggy area, clear leaves from any nearby yard drain, and stay off the wet ground so it does not compact further while it dries.

how to install drainage in yard?

Drainage goes in before the lawn surface is finished. LSU AgCenter's turf establishment guidance places in-ground drainage during site preparation, after the area is cleared and contoured and before the final grade is set and rolled. On an established lawn, the turf over the work area has to be disturbed and repaired afterward.
