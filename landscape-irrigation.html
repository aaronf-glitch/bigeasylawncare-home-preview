Local Conditions

Why Does Irrigation Work Differently in New Orleans?

Irrigation works differently in New Orleans because heavy rain and dry spells often arrive in the same season, and many local clay soils take water in slowly. LSU AgCenter advises watering clay soils in several short, back-to-back cycles and running irrigation early in the morning, so water soaks in and leaves dry before evening.

  • Rain counts toward the total.LSU AgCenter says lawns generally need about one inch of rain or water per week to stay green.
  • Clay takes it slowly.On clay, stop when runoff starts, wait, then resume until the moist zone is 3 to 4 inches deep.
  • Humidity raises disease risk.Morning watering limits how long leaves stay wet, which reduces the potential for fungal disease.

How Should Irrigation Zones Be Planned in New Orleans?

Irrigation zones in New Orleans work best when plants with similar water needs share a zone. LSU AgCenter recommends grouping plant materials this way, since deep-rooted, drought-resistant plants need water less often than shallow-rooted ones, while shade, slope, and soil type also change how much each area should receive.

FAQ

What Do People Ask About Irrigation Systems in New Orleans?

what is an irrigation system?

An irrigation system delivers water to a lawn or landscape on a schedule, so plants get moisture between rains. In New Orleans, a good system is planned around zones, with turf, shrubs, and shaded beds watered separately, because LSU AgCenter notes that plants with different root depths need water at different intervals.

how much does a sprinkler system cost?

The cost of a sprinkler or irrigation system in New Orleans depends on the size of the property, how many areas need water, and the scope of the work. Every yard is different, so the most accurate number comes from a free on-site estimate, which we provide with no obligation.

what is a drip irrigation system?

Drip irrigation is a form of micro-irrigation that delivers small amounts of water slowly to the roots of individual plants or groups of plants. LSU AgCenter says drip and low-volume spray emitters can reduce landscape water use by up to 50 percent compared with sprinklers, and they work best for trees, shrubs, and ground covers.

how often should you water your lawn?

Most lawns need about one inch of rain or water per week to stay green, according to LSU AgCenter. In a New Orleans summer, that usually means adding 0.5 to 0.75 inch every 7 to 10 days when rain falls short, and possibly a third watering a week in hot, dry spells.

when is the best time to water your lawn?

Early morning is the best time to water a lawn. LSU AgCenter explains that morning irrigation limits how long grass blades stay wet, reduces water lost to evaporation, and takes advantage of better water pressure. In humid New Orleans weather, that shorter wet period lowers the risk of fungal disease.

how long should sprinklers run in each zone?

Run time depends on how fast each zone puts out water, so measure it. LSU AgCenter suggests setting tuna cans around the area to check output in inches per hour. On clay soil, water in short, back-to-back cycles, pausing when runoff starts, until the moist zone reaches 3 to 4 inches deep.

will watering dead grass bring it back?

Watering cannot revive grass that has truly died, but brown grass is often dormant rather than dead. LSU AgCenter says well-rooted turf can usually stay drought-dormant for three to four weeks. After that, wetting the soil down to 5 inches can keep it alive, though it may not fully green up.

how long does it take to install a sprinkler system?

How long irrigation work takes depends on the size of the property, the number of areas that need water, and the scope of the job. We look at the lawn and beds during the free on-site estimate and explain the scope before any work starts, so you know exactly what is planned.
