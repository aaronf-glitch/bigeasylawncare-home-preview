Revamp Your Yard: 6 Landscaping Ideas for Winter

September 14, 2023·9 min read·By the Big Easy Lawn Care team

Have you ever gazed at your winter view and longed for a pop of color? Or maybe you’ve yearned for some warmth on those chilly, snowy nights? If so, know that you’re not alone.

With these 6 winter landscaping ideas, your lackluster yard can transform into a year-round oasis. Imagine stepping outside and being greeted by the lush green of evergreen trees, even in the middle of December. Envision snuggling up with loved ones around an outdoor fire pit on a frosty evening.

Join us as we explore how vibrant containers can bring cheer and why mulch is more than just a pretty sight – it also provides protection! Don’t let the thrill of creating something beautiful go into hibernation during the winter—let’s embrace it together! Are you ready to turn your outdoor space into a stunning winter paradise? Keep reading.

1. Plant Evergreen Trees and Shrubs

Think about adding a splash of green to your winter landscape. Evergreens, with their year-round color and texture, can transform your yard into an inviting oasis even during the chilliest months.

There’s something magical about evergreens in winter. When other plants have lost their leaves and gone dormant, evergreen trees and shrubs stand tall and vibrant. They’re nature’s way of reminding us that life goes on, despite the frosty weather.

The Benefits of Planting Evergreens

Beyond providing a pop of color in an otherwise drab season, evergreens serve several practical purposes too. First off, they give birds shelter from cold winds while offering them berries for food during lean times.

They also act as windbreaks reducing heating costs by up to 30%. A row of densely planted spruces or pines can make a world difference when it comes to blocking icy gusts from reaching your home.

Selecting the Right Variety

At Big Easy Lawn Care in New Orleans, we suggest selecting native plants like the Southern Magnolia or Eastern Red Cedar that can thrive in our climate. 

If you want variety but need help deciding which ones are best suited for your space just ask us. Our experts here at Big Easy Lawn Care love sharing our knowledge about what works best in our unique local environment.

So, why not add some evergreen trees and shrubs to your winter landscape? They’re a great way to bring life, color, and texture into your garden all year round.

2. Create a Winter Garden

Winter doesn’t mean you have to say goodbye to your vibrant garden. There are plenty of plants that thrive in cold weather, transforming your outdoor space into a winter wonderland.

Pansies: The Winter Warriors

Pansies aren’t just pretty faces; they’re hardy flowers that can withstand frost and snow. Planting these blooms will add pops of color even on the gloomiest winter days.

The Beauty of Ornamental Cabbage and Kale

No one said cabbages couldn’t be elegant. Ornamental cabbage and kale come in shades from pink to purple, making them a great way to brighten up your landscape when other plants are dormant.

Avoiding Common Pitfalls

Gardening during winter isn’t without its challenges but fear not – we’re here with some practical advice. Ensure the soil is sufficiently aerated, as an excessive amount of water can cause root decay or destroy delicate sprouts.

Mulch for Moisture Retention

Mulching is key. Mulching is essential for keeping the soil damp and also provides a protective layer against extreme temperatures while warding off unwanted weeds.
Remember though – mulch should never touch plant stems as this could cause them harm.

At Big Easy Lawn Care, we strive to give realistic, straightforward counsel to aid you in taking full advantage of your garden – even when the weather is cold.

3. Install Outdoor Lighting

The dark winter months may seem long, but they also offer a unique opportunity to show off your landscaping ideas for cold climates. One such way is by installing outdoor lighting. It’s not just about making things visible; it’s about creating an ambiance that can turn your garden into a magical winter wonderland.

Lights can emphasize the natural beauty of bare trees or highlight the structure of your home and other elements like statues or water features. 

Pick The Right Lights

You’ll need to decide what kind of lights will work best for you: solar-powered, LED, low-voltage electric? Each type has its own pros and cons depending on what you require and prefer. While solar lights may be energy-efficient and simple to install, they could lack brightness or dependability on cloudy days which are frequent during the wintertime.

Placement Is Key

Next comes placement – knowing where to put those lights is half the battle won. A well-lit pathway ensures safety when walking around at night whereas spotlighting key architectural features adds depth and drama. Just make sure not to create light pollution which could disrupt wildlife habits or annoy neighbors.

Maintenance Matters Too

Regularly check that all bulbs are working, especially after heavy snowfall. Clean any dirt build-up from the fixtures to maintain their brightness. Adjust the positioning if necessary due to weather changes affecting the light direction. Always prioritize safety when dealing with outdoor electricity.

4. Add Colorful Containers

Brighten up your outdoor space with colorful containers filled with seasonal flowers or plants.

When winter sets in, your outdoor space can start to look a bit bleak. But there’s a simple and effective way to add some life back into it – colorful containers. These are easy-to-manage elements that make your garden pop with color even during the gloomiest months.

Firstly, consider what you want to plant. Seasonal flowers or plants, like pansies and ornamental cabbages, not only survive but thrive in cold weather conditions. They offer vibrant colors that brighten up any space.

Selecting Your Containers

The next step is picking out containers that complement these plants while adding their own splash of color. Ceramic pots provide excellent insulation for roots against frosty temperatures and come in an array of hues and designs.

Metal containers give off a sleek modern vibe but may need extra care as they conduct cold quickly. Whichever material you choose, ensure it has adequate drainage holes to prevent waterlogging.

Placement

The final piece of this puzzle lies in arranging these colorful additions effectively around your yard or patio area. Container placement strategies vary based on factors such as available sunlight and the overall aesthetic appeal desired from different viewing angles.

Incorporating colored containers into your landscape design will bring visual interest throughout those chilly months when most other plants lie dormant.

5. Incorporate Mulch

One way to help keep your garden looking lively during the colder months is by using mulch. Mulching can be an incredibly useful tool to help maintain the health and appearance of your garden in winter.

Mulch, like this premium one from Gardeners, acts as a protective blanket for your plants. It helps retain moisture in the soil, essential when frigid temperatures start freezing everything around.

The Magic Behind Mulching

Much like how we bundle up in warm clothes during winter, our plants need their own version of protection too. And that’s where mulch comes into play.

It protects plant roots from freezing and reduces water evaporation from the soil surface. Think of it as a warm sweater and a refreshing drink at the same time. Just as we wear sun hats on sunny days to protect ourselves from harmful UV rays, mulching does something similar by preventing weed growth which competes with desirable plants for nutrients and sunlight.

Picking The Right Type Of Mulch For Winter

Different types of mulches are available depending upon what you need them for. Organic options such as wood chips or straw work well because they break down over time adding beneficial organic matter back into the soil. 

So, give your plants the gift of mulch this winter. They’ll thank you by thriving in spring and making your garden a neighborhood envy.

6. Build a Fire Pit

A fire pit can be the crowning glory of your winter landscape. Not only is a fire pit an aesthetically pleasing addition to your winter landscape, but it also has practical applications.

Choosing Your Spot and Materials

The first step in building a fire pit is choosing the right spot. Make sure it’s far from flammable structures, overhanging branches, or dry grasses that could catch on fire easily. For materials, bricks or natural stones are great options due to their heat resistance properties.

Digging and Laying Bricks

Once you’ve picked your spot and gathered materials, start by digging a hole around 1 foot deep and up to 4 feet wide depending on how big you want your pit to be. Then begin laying bricks or stones at the bottom for added stability before stacking them along the edges.

Making It Safe

Safety should always come first when dealing with open flames. Remember to have a bucket of water nearby in case of emergencies. Use metal mesh screens to prevent sparks. Follow the recommendation of NFPA (National Fire Protection Association). Always keep children supervised when near the fire pit.

Maintenance Tips

  • Clean out ash regularly, so it doesn’t build up excessively inside your pit.
  • In case of heavy rain or snowfall cover it with waterproof covers which you can get from any home improvement store.
  • To prolong its life make sure that every once in a while check if any brick has loosened up over time and fix it.

Building a fire pit isn’t just about having a cozy place to sit around during the cold months. Building a fire pit is not just about having a cozy place to sit during colder months, but also about creating an outdoor area where you can gather with friends, make memories, or simply enjoy some alone time under the night sky.

6 Winter Landscaping Ideas: FAQs

What can you do with landscape in the winter?

You can plant evergreens, design a cold-weather garden, use outdoor lighting to emphasize features, and add colorful containers for a pop of color.

How can I make my yard look better in the winter?

To enhance your yard’s appeal during winter, incorporate mulch to retain soil moisture and build a fire pit for added warmth and coziness.

Is it OK to landscape in winter?

Absolutely. Winter landscaping allows you to utilize plants that thrive in colder conditions and provides year-round interest in your outdoor space.

How do you plan a winter garden?

Select plants that are frost-tolerant like pansies or ornamental cabbage. Use mulch for protection against chill and maintain adequate watering routines.

In conclusion, your winter landscape doesn’t have to be a dull, lifeless space. With these six innovative landscaping ideas, you can transform your outdoor area into a year-round oasis of beauty and warmth. From evergreen trees and shrubs to winter flowers in vibrant containers, your garden can be transformed into a magical wonderland with endless possibilities.
